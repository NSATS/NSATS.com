NSATS publications

White papers

These papers explain the scientific background and practical considerations involved in NSATS’s computing and positioning technologies. The Quantum papers examine classical computing, information preservation and effective storage capacity. The Geomagnetism papers examine calculated positions, reference evidence, operating conditions and security.

Choose Read online to read a paper or Request the PDF to request a copy. Both routes require verification with a work or academic email address.

Quantum

White paper · PUBLIC

Applying Quantum Mechanics to Classical Computing

Ankur Srivastava · Version 1.1 · October 2026
Revision · 9 November 2026

This paper considers an NSATS implementation as a complete computing task, including the memory, storage and transfers needed around a calculation. Its assessment protocol sets out how to compare the implementation with a defined baseline and examine the result against the organisation’s acceptance criteria. The emphasis is on understanding what the evidence establishes for the task being assessed.

Topics Reversible Computing · Chip Design · Temporal Compression

White paper · PUBLIC

Reversible Computing: Preserving Information Through Computation

Ankur Srivastava · Version 1.1 · August 2026
Revision · 9 November 2026

This paper examines why recovering earlier information and preserving information throughout a reversible computation are different properties. It considers the resources and dependencies that accompany retained information, then follows their consequences through storage, working memory, communication, security and data centres.

Topics Reversible Computing · Processors and Internal Buses · Memory and RAM

White paper · PUBLIC

Temporal Compression: Understanding Effective Storage Capacity

Ankur Srivastava · Version 1.1 · June 2026
Revision · 9 November 2026

This paper examines how the processing required when data arrives, is compressed again and is retrieved relates to the space saved while it is retained. It considers when greater effective capacity within a fixed physical resource could meet retrieval requirements at a lower whole-system cost. Compression is operator-defined, with the setting and upper limit considered alongside the data, storage device and implementation. The paper’s numerical example is hypothetical arithmetic, not a measured result or an NSATS specification.

Topics Temporal Compression · Memory and RAM · Reversible Computing

Geomagnetism

These papers explain how magnetic measurements are used to calculate position and how an organisation can assess a particular implementation. Examples labelled “synthetic sample — not a measurement” illustrate methods and record formats; they do not establish measured performance.

White paper · PUBLIC

Geomagnetic positioning without live satellite signals: what is measured and what is calculated

Ankur Srivastava · Version 1.1 · October 2026
Revision · 10 November 2026

A magnetic sensor measures a field; a positioning implementation uses those measurements to calculate location. This paper explains that distinction and the role of an independent reference when assessing the calculated position. It also examines the installation, operating conditions and application requirements that define what an assessment needs to establish.

Topics: Geomagnetic Positioning · Satellite-independent Positioning · Evaluation Methods

White paper · PUBLIC

Evaluating a geomagnetic positioning implementation: reference tracks, deviation and the start-point convention

Ankur Srivastava · Version 1.1 · August 2026
Revision · 10 November 2026

A positioning assessment compares calculated positions with a reference and defines how the difference between them is reported. This paper explains reference tracks, positions expressed relative to a common start point, and the distinction between deviation at an individual point and a summary of a run. Its synthetic examples illustrate the method without supplying measurement evidence.

Topics: Evaluation Methods · Geomagnetic Positioning · Installation and Calibration

White paper · PUBLIC

Movement cases for validation: vessels, aircraft, land vehicles and the laboratory

Ankur Srivastava · Version 1.1 · June 2026
Revision · 10 November 2026

A vessel, an aircraft and a slow-moving vehicle place different demands on a positioning assessment. This paper examines how speed, the spacing of observations, sensor mounting and the reference source define each case, alongside a laboratory series intended to examine repeatability. The synthetic tables and figures illustrate the assessment structure and do not report completed trials.

Topics: Evaluation Methods · Operating Environments · Geomagnetic Positioning

White paper · PUBLIC

Magnetic disturbance and noise: what filtering is expected to handle and what it is not

Ankur Srivastava · Version 1.1 · April 2026
Revision · 10 November 2026

Motors, steel structures, electrical loads and changes in the surrounding magnetic field can affect the conditions in which sensors operate. This paper examines the expected role of NSATS’s proprietary noise filtering and explains why its effect on positioning must be assessed under stated conditions. It connects those conditions to sensor installation, calibration and checks when equipment or surroundings change.

Topics: Operating Environments · Installation and Calibration · Evaluation Methods

White paper · PUBLIC

Security considerations for geomagnetic positioning without live GPS satellite signals

Ankur Srivastava · Version 1.1 · February 2026
Revision · 10 November 2026

A positioning function that does not rely on live satellite signals still needs a security assessment covering its sensors, processing and the application using its output. This paper examines unauthorised access, disruption and tampering, and explains why agreement with a reference position does not establish security. The assessment must address the particular implementation, its operating conditions and the threats relevant to its intended use.

Topics: Security and Resilience · Satellite-independent Positioning · Installation and Calibration
