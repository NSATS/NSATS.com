Getting started

The magnetometer software development kit (SDK) interface design describes how an application would connect to equipment, establish authorised access and acquire a reading from eight separately identified channels. This walkthrough introduces that sequence and the information needed to interpret a result.

It is a reading exercise that requires no software installation or equipment connection. The commands are illustrative design forms, not executable instructions, and the acquisition records do not define how geographic position is calculated.

Connection, access and acquisition

Design documentation · Draft 2 · 9 October 2026

A connection establishes communication with the intended device; device login establishes the permissions needed for protected operations. The application then inspects the configuration before requesting a reading, keeping each channel’s identity and status with its result. Processing, calibration and self-tests are separate operations with their own prerequisites.

Authentication belongs to the magnetometer and is separate from MyNSATS sign-in. The website does not authenticate or control equipment. An implementation would need a supported device reference, adapter and authentication mechanism before the design sequence could be used with hardware.

Walkthrough at a glance

The sequence below shows what each illustrative request is intended to establish. Read it alongside the detailed walkthrough; where a device capability or record field has not been defined, retain that uncertainty rather than assuming a default.

Connectconnect DEVICE_REFERENCECommunication is established with the intended device; connection alone does not grant measurement access.
Log inloginThe device mechanism confirms access and permissions. Stop if that mechanism is unavailable.
InspectdescribeIdentify the eight-channel mapping, representation, timing, applicable calibration and effective configuration. Keep missing metadata explicit.
Read onceread once channels=allEach of the eight channels has its own identity and status; a partial acquisition identifies missing data.

Exercises 1–3 and the averaging exercise

Interpreting the first reading

The hardware and data interface defines the information a record needs, while the command reference describes the operations that request it. Together, they distinguish an individual channel from the complete acquisition and a missing observation from a numeric zero. Representation, units and timing must come from the device description; they cannot be inferred from an example value.

Exercises 1–3 follow connection and login, an eight-channel reading and a partial acquisition. For each step, identify the information required before the request and the status returned afterwards. The separate averaging exercise shows how included, rejected and missing observations affect the result for each channel.

Responsibilities after acquisition

The receiving application decides how to display or retain the result, while the session and adapter keep it associated with the correct device and configuration. These responsibilities also matter when communication stops: closing a local connection does not establish what the equipment completed or whether an active operation has stopped.
