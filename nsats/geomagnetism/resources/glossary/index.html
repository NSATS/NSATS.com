Geomagnetism glossary

Geomagnetic positioning supplies location information to equipment and applications. The terms below distinguish that output from direction, explain how it passes into a system, and describe the information needed to assess its use.

Geomagnetic positioning

Determining location using Earth’s magnetic field. A magnetic measurement describes the field’s strength or direction at a place; the position output identifies a geographic location. NSATS’s technology provides latitude and longitude for use by the receiving equipment or application.

Positioning from Earth’s magnetic field →

Position and heading

Position identifies where equipment is located. Heading describes the direction it faces relative to a stated reference, such as magnetic north or geographic north. Direction of travel describes its movement and can differ from its heading. These quantities serve distinct purposes and should not be treated as interchangeable.

GPS

The Global Positioning System, which uses satellite broadcasts to support positioning and related functions. A receiver calculates its output from the broadcasts it receives, and the application determines how to use that information. NSATS’s geomagnetic position calculation does not rely on live GPS broadcasts.

Positioning without GPS satellite signals →

GNSS

Global Navigation Satellite System: the general category of satellite navigation systems that includes GPS. A receiver may support one or more systems, so its specification must identify which it uses. The GPS-independence statements on these NSATS pages concern GPS specifically; the category name does not extend that claim to every satellite system.

Position update

A newly supplied position output. An application may use it immediately or retain it with an event, in which case the record must keep the position associated with that event and its time. A retained output remains an earlier position rather than becoming a new observation when it is used again.

Reference position

Position information used to assess another position output. The comparison must concern the same object and observation time, with the source of the reference and its uncertainty recorded. Agreement with a reference is meaningful only when the method of obtaining and comparing the two positions is clear.

How positioning is evaluated →

Embedded implementation

A positioning function incorporated within equipment designed for a particular purpose. Its installation, enclosure, electrical supply and connections form part of the device’s design. Assessment must consider how the function operates alongside the equipment’s other activities, rather than treating it as an isolated component.

Geomagnetism implementation →

System integration

Connecting the positioning function to the hardware and software that use its output. The receiving application must interpret the coordinates and preserve their association with the relevant equipment, time or event. The design also defines how the application responds when a suitable position is unavailable.

Positioning resilience

The ability of a positioning arrangement to support its intended task under relevant disturbances and interruptions. This includes the application’s response to uncertain or unavailable information and its behaviour when useful information returns. Independence from a particular signal source does not establish the resilience or security of the complete system.

Position update rate

How often a positioning function supplies a new position. This differs from the age of the information when it is used: transfer, processing or waiting in the receiving application can delay its use. Both the supply of updates and their age matter when assessing whether the output suits the task.

Setup and initialisation

Preparation before equipment begins its task. Setup establishes the selected hardware and software arrangement; initialisation brings it into the state needed to start operating. The required steps depend on the implementation. An evaluation record should distinguish this preparation from restarts or configuration changes made during the work.
