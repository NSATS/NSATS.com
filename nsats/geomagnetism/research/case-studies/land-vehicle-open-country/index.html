A land vehicle at 10 km/h on open country

Summary

This case is built on a synthetic sample — not a measurement — to fix the record’s layout; it is published as a labelled illustration and is replaced by recorded runs as they are approved. The land-vehicle case concerns a short distance between observations in a task where platform noise is high. The synthetic record illustrates a reference track, calculated positions and per-point deviations, with field columns before and after filtering. It provides no measured positioning or filtering result and establishes no urban or indoor behaviour.

What this case aims to show

The assessment question concerns per-point deviation for a slow land vehicle when the distance moved per interval is short and platform noise is high. In this synthetic sample — not a measurement — the track, deviation chart and field columns illustrate how to read that case. The high-noise setting defines the assessment context; it is not an observation established by these generated rows.

The arrangement

In the synthetic sample — not a measurement — the platform is a land vehicle on open country, with a satellite GPS receiver on the same platform as the reference. The sample represents 8 sensors and identifies the software version as SAMPLE. Its reference and calculated positions are metres north and east of the start point, identified only by its gps_loc_code.

The task

The synthetic sample — not a measurement — contains one run, G-SAMPLE-006, with a nominal speed of 10 km/h. Its duration is 600 s, with an interval of 30 s and 21 points including the start. The reference position and each sensor’s calculated position are paired at each point.

Both field columns appear beside those positions in the point table. “Raw field” is the magnitude before NSATS’s proprietary noise filtering; “Filtered field” is the magnitude after it, in nanotesla (nT). These synthetic sample values illustrate the two columns, not a measured reduction in noise or an assessment of the filtering method.

How to read the results

At each point the arrangement calculates a position afresh. Deviation is the horizontal distance between the calculated position and the reference position at that point. As a percentage, it is that distance divided by the distance moved since the previous point, multiplied by 100. The start point has no previous point and is excluded.

All values here belong to a synthetic sample — not a measurement. Point 0 is excluded from both metre and percentage summaries. The point table retains the start row, with its undefined percentage shown as a dash. Read each horizontal deviation with the step length at the same point; the field columns describe field magnitude, not position error.

The track uses metres east and north of the start point. The percentage chart uses point index horizontally, with sensor traces identified by their line styles and labels as well as colour. Each point’s elapsed time is retained in the table.
