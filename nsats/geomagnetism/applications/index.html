Geomagnetic positioning applications

Position information supports equipment, inspection and operational tasks in many settings. NSATS geomagnetic positioning is developed and licensed now: it determines location using Earth’s magnetic field, and licensees can incorporate it into their own chips, embedded hardware, devices and systems. NSATS sells technology licences, not chips. Its technology helps licensees manufacture their own chips. Geomagnetic positioning’s intended directions include underground, on the surface, in the air, underwater, indoors and in dense urban areas, as the application directions below show.

Mines and underground operations

Underground equipment and inspection devices need to associate activity with a place. Geomagnetic positioning provides a location input for mine or tunnel equipment, with the intended task determining the required precision and frequency of position updates. A licensee’s assessment should reflect the route, surrounding infrastructure and movement encountered during that work.

A location input is information supplied to application software about where equipment or an observation is situated. An inspection record needs to show whether that place was examined or merely passed through.

Mines, tunnels and underground operations →

Industrial and warehouse robotics

Mobile industrial equipment uses location information within a wider system for planning and carrying out tasks. Geomagnetic positioning is designed to provide that input for equipment operating indoors in factories or warehouses. The practical question for a licensee is how the position input contributes to the task in a setting that includes machinery, structures and changing activity.

Reassigning work changes which operation a robot should perform at a location, even when its destination stays the same.

Industrial and warehouse robotics →

Road, rail and surface mobility

Surface vehicles and inspection equipment may need position information along a route or within a site, including in dense urban areas. Uses include associating observations with places and supporting an operator’s awareness of location. The relevant evaluation depends on the vehicle, its movement and the application that will use the information within the surrounding system.

A journey record places each observation in the sequence of visits, including returns to places encountered earlier.

Road, rail and surface mobility →

Aircraft and aerial inspection

In the airborne setting introduced above, geomagnetic positioning provides an additional positioning input for aircraft and drones. Aerial inspection, for example, needs to relate collected observations to a location. A licensee’s implementation should be assessed on the intended platform and under representative flight conditions, with the useful position requirement defined by the application.

In aerial inspection, the location of the carrying aircraft and the identity of the structure being examined serve different roles in the observation record.

Aircraft, drones and aerial inspection →

Emergency response and remote operations

Equipment used in remote or disrupted settings can have a need for local position information. Geomagnetic positioning provides a location input for devices supporting field teams or remote inspection. The full application must connect that information to an operational task, including a separate means of reporting it when remote communication is required.

A report received after field work has ended still needs the observation time to establish when its location was relevant.

Emergency response and remote operations →

Logistics and asset location

Asset devices can use position information to associate an item with a place. Geomagnetic positioning can be incorporated within such a device, with integration shaped by its enclosure, movement and power requirements. Reporting the information to another system is an additional part of the application and its communications design.

An asset identifier distinguishes the item through its movements, while each location entry describes a particular event in that item’s history.

Logistics and asset location →

Marine and subsea systems

For the marine and underwater settings introduced above, geomagnetic positioning provides a location input within inspection equipment. Underwater equipment may need to relate an observation to a place along its route. A licensee’s evaluation begins with the intended platform and environment, followed by an assessment tied to the task the equipment needs to perform.

Underwater equipment can travel to a work area, inspect while moving or stationary, and return, with location serving a different purpose at each stage.

Marine and subsea positioning →

Infrastructure inspection

Inspection findings become more useful when they can be associated with a location. Geomagnetic positioning provides that input within equipment inspecting tunnels, enclosed facilities and other infrastructure, whether underground, indoors or in dense urban areas. The implementation should be considered against the site, the route and the level of location detail needed to find and act on an observation.

A maintenance status can change while the original inspection finding remains in the work record.

Infrastructure inspection and maintenance →

Consumer devices and wearables

Portable devices are an application direction for manufacturers incorporating location functionality under licence, including devices used indoors and in dense urban areas. The relevant questions include how positioning serves the user’s task and fits the device’s power, size and interface requirements. A licensee’s evaluation follows its device and intended operating environment.

A portable application may associate a place with a deliberate user action, such as marking an activity for later review.

Consumer devices and wearables →
