Marine and subsea positioning

Marine and subsea systems are an intended direction for NSATS geomagnetic positioning, which determines location using Earth’s magnetic field. The technology is designed to provide useful location information for a defined vessel or underwater task.

On this page

  1. Inspection use
  2. Feasibility for the platform
  3. A question for underwater inspection

Inspection use

Underwater inspection equipment may need to associate observations with a place along its work. A positioning input supports that task within the wider vehicle or inspection system. Its value depends on the information required and the conditions represented by the operation.

Underwater inspection work can involve equipment moving along an asset or remaining at a selected part of it. The inspection record needs to identify the subject being examined, such as a section of a structure, separately from the equipment carrying out the work. That distinction determines which observations belong together when the inspection is reviewed later.

Feasibility for the platform

A licensee’s work begins with platform-specific feasibility:

  1. Define the equipment, environment, movement and integration requirements.
  2. Select an assessment and a comparison reference.
  3. Evaluate on the intended platform in the underwater setting.

Platform-specific feasibility examines whether the intended equipment arrangement can meet the underwater task’s requirements before committing to its implementation. A comparison reference supplies an independently obtained position against which the implementation’s output can be assessed.

A question for underwater inspection

For the inspection task described above, geomagnetic positioning supplies the location information. The licensee’s platform-specific feasibility work and evaluation establish how the input serves that use on its own equipment.

In an illustrative inspection of a submerged structure, the equipment travels along the asset and then holds station to examine a joint. The record identifies the travelling observations separately from those collected while stationary.

For underwater equipment, deployment covers getting the equipment into position to begin work; the inspection period produces observations associated with that work; return ends that collection period and brings the equipment back. The record identifies the initial placement during deployment, the observations collected during inspection and the end of collection at return.
