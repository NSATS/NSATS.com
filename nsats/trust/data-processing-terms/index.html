Who these terms are for and when they apply

These terms apply when a supplier processes personal data on behalf of National Scientific AI & Technical Systems Ltd (NSATS Ltd), registered in the Dubai International Financial Centre. NSATS Ltd is the controller and the supplier is the processor. The NSATS Swiss office and NSATS USA office act for the same controller.

The supplier and NSATS Ltd must sign these terms and complete the engagement annex before processing begins under a purchase order or contract.

The layer that applies is decided by the people whose personal data is processed: the EU layer for people in the European Union; the Swiss layer for people in Switzerland; the DIFC layer for people in the United Arab Emirates and for any processing carried out in the UAE; the rest-of-world layer for everyone else. Where more than one layer applies to an engagement, each applies to the data it covers. Applicable mandatory law continues to govern the processing.

Common terms (all layers)

The engagement annex records the subject matter, duration, nature and purpose of the processing, the categories of personal data and the people concerned, and NSATS Ltd’s rights and obligations.

The supplier must follow NSATS Ltd’s documented instructions. Where applicable law requires other processing, the supplier must tell NSATS Ltd before acting, unless that law prohibits notification. It must tell NSATS Ltd immediately if an instruction infringes applicable data protection law.

Personnel authorised to process the data must be bound by confidentiality. The supplier must apply the technical and organisational security measures recorded in the annex, appropriate to the risks of the processing.

A sub-processor requires NSATS Ltd’s prior written authorisation and a written agreement imposing the same data-protection obligations. The supplier remains responsible to NSATS Ltd for the sub-processor’s performance. [COUNSEL: Select specific or general authorisation and complete the required notice period, authorisation procedure and objection procedure in the applicable clauses.]

The supplier must assist NSATS Ltd with requests from people exercising their data-protection rights and with security, breach notification and data-protection impact assessments. The annex records the measures and information needed for that assistance.

The supplier must notify NSATS Ltd of a personal-data breach without undue delay and in any event within 48 hours of becoming aware of it. This contractual deadline does not permit delay where applicable law or the incorporated clauses require earlier notification. [COUNSEL: Confirm the contractual deadline and the notification contact and procedure before signature.]

At the end of the services, the supplier must return or delete the personal data, as NSATS Ltd chooses, and delete retained copies unless applicable law requires their retention. It must provide the information and access needed to demonstrate compliance and assist with audits and inspections.

Liability is governed by the supplier terms, subject to mandatory law and the precedence of any incorporated standard clauses. [COUNSEL: Confirm that the supplier terms’ liability, third-party-rights and governing-law provisions do not restrict rights or remedies under the incorporated clauses.]

DIFC layer (UAE)

The supplier must meet the processor obligations in Article 24 of the DIFC Data Protection Law No. 5 of 2020, as amended. The Commissioner’s Article 24 processor clauses, identified in the engagement annex, are incorporated by reference under Article 24(8).

[COUNSEL: Identify the dated processor-clause text from the Commissioner’s Article 24 Contract Clauses and DIFC Abbreviated SCCs, confirm its incorporation and complete its options and engagement details. Do not substitute transfer clauses for the processor clauses.]

Article 41 requires the processor to notify the controller of a personal-data breach without undue delay. [COUNSEL: Confirm the article and sub-article references to the current consolidated text of the Law.] Transfers out of the DIFC require an adequate destination under Article 26 or appropriate safeguards under Article 27.

Where contractual transfer safeguards are required, the applicable DIFC transfer standard contractual clauses must be identified and incorporated in the engagement annex before the transfer. [COUNSEL: Confirm the destination, parties, transfer mechanism, applicable clause version and completed annexes.]

DIFC law governs this layer and disputes under it are subject to the DIFC Courts, subject to mandatory law and any overriding rights under the incorporated clauses.

EU layer

The standard contractual clauses in the Annex to Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/915 are incorporated by reference for processing under Article 28 GDPR, with NSATS Ltd as controller and the supplier as processor. Their annexes form part of the signed engagement.

[COUNSEL: Confirm that this instrument applies to the engagement; select its GDPR options, decide whether the optional docking clause applies, and complete the parties, processing, security and sub-processor annexes. Choose the EU Member State law governing this layer and the competent courts, without altering the standard clauses or restricting statutory rights.]

Personal data of people in the EU handled on NSATS’s own systems is held in Switzerland. Data of everyone else, apart from people in Switzerland, is held in the DIFC. These terms do not authorise a change in those arrangements; any new transfer requires a privacy-notice update first.

The processor clauses do not themselves authorise international transfers. For a transfer to a supplier outside the EU/EEA that requires contractual safeguards, the applicable clauses in the Annex to Decision (EU) 2021/914 are incorporated by reference where their conditions for use are met. Module two covers a controller-to-processor transfer; module three covers a processor-to-processor transfer.

Swiss layer

Processing for this layer must meet Article 9 of the Swiss Federal Act on Data Protection. Personal data of people in Switzerland handled on NSATS’s own systems is held in Switzerland. The supplier must notify NSATS Ltd of a data-security breach as quickly as possible under Article 24(3).

The EU layer’s processor obligations apply contractually with the Swiss adjustments recorded in the engagement annex. [COUNSEL: Set out the FADP adjustments and confirm the processor agreement’s compliance with Article 9; do not describe adapted processor terms as FDPIC-recognised transfer clauses.]

Where a Swiss transfer requires standard contractual safeguards, the applicable modules of Decision (EU) 2021/914 are incorporated with the adaptations in the FDPIC’s recognition guidance. For the Swiss regime, the annex identifies the FDPIC, interprets GDPR references as FADP references where appropriate, and preserves the right of people in Switzerland to bring proceedings there.

Swiss law and Swiss courts govern this layer, without restricting mandatory rights of data subjects. [COUNSEL: Specify the competent Swiss courts and complete the Swiss adaptations. Where the GDPR also applies, retain its supervisory authority, governing-law and forum requirements alongside the Swiss requirements.]

Rest of world

For engagements covered by this layer, the EU layer’s processor obligations apply as contractual terms, alongside applicable local law. For the United States, this includes applicable state laws.

[COUNSEL: Confirm this layer’s scope and its relationship with the DIFC default stated above. The proposed governing law is DIFC, unless counsel specifies otherwise; confirm the competent courts. Preserve any mandatory EU or Swiss requirements and any separate law or forum required by transfer clauses. Do not present contractual use of EU processor obligations as extending GDPR statutory jurisdiction.]

Annex per engagement

The supplier completes the annex for NSATS Ltd to agree before signature. It forms part of the engagement and is not published on this page. It records:

  • the parties, their controller or processor roles, the purchase order or contract, and the applicable layers;
  • the subject matter, duration, nature and purpose of processing, categories of personal data and data subjects, and the controller’s rights and obligations;
  • the processing locations, instructions and any permitted transfers, with the operative standard clauses identified by title, version and official source;
  • the security measures and the assistance required for rights requests, breaches and impact assessments;
  • the authorised sub-processors and the agreed authorisation and change procedures;
  • the breach-notification contact and procedure, and the arrangements for return or deletion;
  • the governing law, courts, clause options and signatures required by each applicable instrument.

[COUNSEL: Complete and reconcile the engagement annex with every annex required by the incorporated instruments, and confirm that incorporation by reference and signature bind both parties before processing starts.]
