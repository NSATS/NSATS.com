Positioning evaluation

A positioning result needs context: what was located, where the equipment operated and how the output was assessed. That context determines which application questions a study can answer.

A result and its operating setting

A licensee’s controlled study and field trial represent different conditions. A report should make the platform and task clear so that a reader can understand what the result says about the intended use of the positioning function.

A platform is the equipment on which the implementation operates. A controlled study holds selected conditions steady while examining a question. A field trial observes equipment in the setting of the intended work, where several conditions may change together. In ordinary engineering evaluation, these approaches answer different questions: an isolated change can be examined deliberately, while a working task reveals combinations of conditions that the equipment must encounter.

When observations differ, a reviewer needs to know what changed between them. A revised device, a different route and a different way of using the output are separate changes. If several occur together, the report needs to distinguish what was observed from the explanation proposed for that observation.

Different uses of position

An inspection record needs to associate a finding with a place. Equipment moving along a route may need repeated position updates. These tasks can require different detail and timing, so a result should be interpreted in relation to the use it was assessed for.

A position update is a newly supplied location, whose arrival must be considered against the moving platform’s task.

A recorded observation concerns the place associated with an event that has already happened. A moving operator’s display concerns the equipment’s present work. Information arriving after the event can still be relevant to reviewing a record, provided its association is clear. The same delay can affect a display differently because the operator may already have moved on.

A positioning study should examine whether its output lets the intended recipient carry out the location-dependent action.

Evidence for a development decision

The positioning evaluation guide explains how a reference, operating conditions and observations form an assessment record. That record helps a reader judge the relevance of the evidence to the equipment or application under consideration.
