Quantum questions and answers

Answers to introductory questions about NSATS Quantum and its licensing model.

In this section

What does NSATS license?

NSATS licenses intellectual property for incorporation into software, chips, devices and systems. NSATS licences enable classical devices to perform operations based on quantum mechanics, at room temperature. The Quantum portfolio includes computational methods, memory and storage technology, quantum-classical buses, networking and security architecture.

In general business practice, an intellectual-property licence gives permission to use protected designs or methods within an agreed scope, including in products the licensee develops. Buying a finished product and obtaining permission to use its underlying designs are different transactions.

Who builds the chips and devices?

Licensees build the software, chips, devices or systems incorporating the licensed intellectual property. NSATS sells technology licences, not chips. Its technology helps licensees manufacture their own chips.

An implementation project includes choosing components, connecting them to the surrounding application and checking the intended behaviour. A licensee can identify the work its own team would carry out and contributions it would need from its own suppliers or integrators. These are responsibilities of the proposed project; they are distinct from identifying the technical function it wants to use.

What does quantum-classical mean here?

The technology applies computational relationships drawn from quantum mechanics through specially designed classical circuitry and computing methods, at room temperature without super-cooling. Quantum-inspired gates are operations on information that form part of those designs.

In ordinary computing, an operation applies a rule to supplied information to obtain a result; an application combines operations to carry out its task.

Classical circuitry means electronic circuits that process electrical signals according to defined logic. Ordinary electronics can operate at ambient temperature, with fans or similar cooling removing the heat produced during operation.

How are storage and RAM different?

Storage holds information for later use; RAM supplies working information to a running program.

A program’s working information can include temporary values as well as data selected from a retained collection.

Temporal compression increases effective capacity in both retained storage and RAM: more data can be stored or retrieved within the same physical storage resource. The physical size of the drive stays unchanged. Classical data is encoded using quantum-mechanical logic, and reversible computing contributes to compression.

The increase depends on the drive, how the licensee uses the technology and where the licensed gates, buses and computational technologies sit in the licensee’s design. For both existing drives and RAM, the licensee adds a specially designed classical chipset or controller built with the licensed technology. It encodes the data and manages the intermediate operations when information is stored or retrieved. The drive or RAM itself remains ordinary classical storage. This is not a software-only upgrade or a drop-in change; integration is part of the licensee’s design.

Effective capacity depends on processing time, not only physical size. A temporal drive is better described by how much data it can process and store per second than by a fixed capacity.

In RAM, computer buses can send and retrieve the encoded data faster in exchanges with processors.

What is the networking arrangement?

Quantum-classical network cards, each containing a quantum-classical chip, at both the sending and receiving ends of a classical network connection enable faster data transfer, while the data passes through ordinary routers in between. This includes long-distance transfer and wireless, optical and wired links. The design covers processing around transmission and receipt, including reconstruction of information at the receiving system.

In ordinary networking, routing directs traffic towards its destination. The systems using the information perform the application work, which can include selecting what to send or applying a calculation to what arrives. A network route and the task served by the exchange therefore describe different aspects of the same transfer.

Quantum-Classical Networking →

Does the network design use a card at both ends?

Yes. The design places a chip-containing quantum-classical network card at both endpoints, with compatibility between the cards and integration into each surrounding system.

Where should a licensing discussion begin?

Describe the intended application, the system in which it would run and the computing function you want to develop. Include the evidence or integration questions that matter to the project.

Separate the application’s requirements from design choices that remain open.
