Doing business with NSATS

A supplier proposal should make it easy to understand the contribution offered and the conditions needed to deliver it.

A supplier proposal is an offer to carry out specified work. It sets out what the supplier would contribute and what the proposal assumes about the work provided by the other organisation.

Describe the requirement

Connect your proposal to the work being discussed. Identify the deliverables, the resources you would provide and the responsibilities that would remain with NSATS.

A deliverable is an identifiable result of the work, such as a completed document or an installed component. Resources are the people, equipment and effort used to produce it. A proposal can describe an activity in detail and still leave its deliverable unclear: “prepare a report” needs a stated subject and purpose before either party can judge whether the requested work has been supplied.

Explain delivery

Set out the proposed schedule, dependencies and acceptance criteria. Distinguish a firm commitment from an estimate and identify decisions that could change the scope.

An estimate expresses an expectation based on the information available; a commitment states what the supplier undertakes to deliver on agreed terms. Where an estimate depends on receiving information or a decision, identify that dependency alongside it. Acceptance criteria describe the properties the delivered work must satisfy. They should be specific enough for someone to check the result against the requirement, rather than relying on an undefined description such as “complete”.

Document the engagement

Proposed practice for review — not adopted company policy.

Before starting work, establish the agreed scope, commercial terms and authority to proceed. Keep changes linked to that agreement so that both organisations understand their effect on delivery.

Authority to proceed should identify who can approve the work, as distinct from who prepares the proposal.

Keep records consistent

Use the relevant engagement or order reference on delivery records and invoices. Direct questions about scope, delivery or payment through the corresponding supplier enquiry route.

The invoicing guidance distinguishes the reference for a charge from the order or agreement to which it relates.
