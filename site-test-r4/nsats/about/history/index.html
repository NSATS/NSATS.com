Our story

NSATS began with the aim of turning ideas from quantum mechanics into usable technology.

From materials to electrical signals

Our starting point was the complexity of quantum mechanics and the practical challenge of using its principles without super-cooling. We first explored materials, looking for a route from the science to useful applications.

The research then turned towards traditional electricity. Electrical signals offered a different way to investigate how relationships described by quantum mechanics could be expressed in classical circuits. Our interest in entanglement helped shape that question: how could related information be represented and used within an electronic system?

Applications in classical electronics

That direction developed into technology for quantum-inspired gates, reversible computing, memory and data movement. A gate performs an operation on information; reversible computing retains data within the circuit, so an output can be traced back to its input and the input recovered from the output. NSATS licences enable classical devices to perform operations based on quantum mechanics, at room temperature. No super-cooling is needed. The technology enables faster processing and lower energy use in classical electronic systems through specially designed classical chipsets. NSATS sells technology licences, not chips. Its technology helps licensees manufacture their own chips.

Temporal compression extends the work to storage and memory, increasing the amount of information that can be stored or retrieved within a physical resource. Geomagnetic location forms a separate portfolio, developed and licensed now, using Earth’s magnetic field as the basis for determining position.

    Quantum-Classical Computing →
  5. 2025

    Quantum Temporal Drive

    Temporal compression increases effective capacity: more data can be stored or retrieved within the same physical storage resource. The physical size of the drive stays unchanged. Classical data is encoded using quantum-mechanical logic, and reversible computing contributes to compression.

    The increase depends on the drive, how the licensee uses the technology and where the licensed gates, buses and computational technologies sit in the licensee’s design. For both existing drives and RAM, the licensee adds a specially designed classical chipset or controller built with the licensed technology. It encodes the data and manages the intermediate operations when information is stored or retrieved. The drive or RAM itself remains ordinary classical storage. This is not a software-only upgrade or a drop-in change; integration is part of the licensee’s design.

    Effective capacity depends on processing time, not only physical size. A temporal drive is better described by how much data it can process and store per second than by a fixed capacity.

    The Temporal Compression page distinguishes retained information from working memory and sets out storage-system implementation considerations.

    Temporal Compression →
